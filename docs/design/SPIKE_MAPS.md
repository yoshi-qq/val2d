# Standard Spike maps

The map selector includes all 13 standard Spike maps listed by the [official VALORANT Wiki](https://wiki.playvalorant.com/en-us/Map).

The ten new layouts were traced from the Wiki tactical images at one metre per sample using the in-game map projection. The Wiki's labeled layouts and area screenshots informed the cover, stairs, ledges and elevated walkways. Walls, plant rectangles, buy barriers and paths are editable in the local map data. The maps use Val2D tiles and props.

## Map data

- `orbs` holds world-space pickup positions.
- A site's optional `upper` rectangles enable planting on the upper floor of a deck, under the same site name as its ground plant zone.
- A deck with `closed_below` has solid container geometry up to its upper floor.
- A wall with `deck_level` begins at that upper floor and leaves lower routes open.
- `ziplines` defines two endpoints, a riding level, an interaction radius and a maximum movement speed in metres per second. An optional `lower` level makes the route a vertical ascender controlled by forward/back input.
- A door object's `trigger` rectangle opens the door while a living player is inside.
- A door object's `close_after` closes it after the specified number of seconds, once the doorway is clear.
- A teleporter's optional `during_buy` flag enables silent crossings and connects the two buy areas on its side.
- `drop_zones` defines the void rectangles and each Spike recovery point.
- `drop_wall_links` binds the rectangle of a destructible switch object to the rectangle of its linked door object. Linked doors start open and close for the rest of the round once their switch breaks.
